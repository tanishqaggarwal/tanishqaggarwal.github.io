This week's three dishes

Every batch below is sized to exactly what the menu eats, plus one spare portion. Scale with a kitchen scale when you can.

Kala Chana Masala

5 plates this week · ~900 g batch
~211 kcal / serving ~170 g on the plate

Beans

  • 275 g dry kala chana, soaked 8–12 hrs
  • ½ tsp salt
  • — water to cover for pressure-cooking

Masala

  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped (~200 g)
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes (~250 g), chopped
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ½–¾ tsp chili powder
  • ¾ tsp garam masala
  • ½ tsp amchur
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 150 ml water

Method

  1. Pressure-cook soaked kala chana until soft — often 35–45 min. Keep ½ cup liquid.
  2. Golden onion in oil → ginger-garlic → tomato → all powders 30 sec.
  3. Add chana + liquid. Simmer 15 min; mash a few for body.
  4. Finish amchur + garam masala.

Darkest, chewiest of the bean curries — the best keeper in the fridge. Batch ~900 g — that is 5 plates of 170 g plus about 50 g spare.

Arhar Dal Tadka

5 plates this week · ~1.05 kg batch
~176 kcal / serving ~200 g on the plate

Dal

  • 250 g arhar / toor dal
  • 750 ml water (about 3 cups)
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 1 small tomato, chopped (optional, in the cooker)

Tadka

  • 1 tbsp ghee or oil
  • ¾ tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 pinch hing (optional)
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped (~100 g)
  • 1 dry red chili or green chili
  • ¼ tsp red chili powder (off heat, optional)

Method

  1. Rinse the dal. Pressure-cook with water, turmeric, salt (+ tomato) until soft; whisk thick and creamy.
  2. Tadka: ghee → cumin → chili → garlic until golden → onion 4 min. Optional chili powder off the heat.
  3. Pour over the dal; simmer 3 min.

Classic North Indian arhar — not sambar. Keep it thick for meal prep. Batch ~1.05 kg — that is 5 plates of 200 g plus about 50 g spare.

Aloo Matar

4 plates this week · ~650 g batch
~96 kcal / serving ~150 g on the plate

In the pan

  • 220 g potatoes, 2 cm cubes
  • 130 g green peas (fresh or frozen)
  • 1½ tbsp oil
  • 1 medium onion, chopped (~125 g)
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 medium tomato, chopped (~125 g)
  • ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • ¼ tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp cumin powder
  • ¾ tsp coriander powder
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp garam masala
  • 125 ml water

Method

  1. Heat oil; cumin 20 sec → onion 6 min → ginger-garlic 1 min → tomato + powders 5 min.
  2. Add the potatoes; stir 2 min. Add water + salt; cover 10–12 min until the potatoes are almost done.
  3. Add the peas; cook 5 min more. Finish with garam masala.

The lightest sabzi here — the peas and the paneer cubes beside it do the protein work. Batch ~650 g — that is 4 plates of 150 g plus about 50 g spare.

Sunday prep

About 60–75 minutes active for three dishes. Do the Saturday-night soaks so Sunday is mostly cook and portion.

Saturday night

~5 min
  1. Soak 275 g kala chana, 8–12 hrs.
  2. Optional: wash the salad veg and store it dry.

Phase 1 — Start the long cooks (in parallel)

~10 min to start · 30–45 min unattended
  1. Kala chana — pressure-cook 35–45 min (slower than kabuli).
  2. Arhar dal — pressure-cook with turmeric + salt.
  3. Put 550 g dry quinoa or rice on (≈ 1.6 kg cooked). The week needs 120 g cooked grain per plate.
  4. While the pots run → Phase 2. Don't stand around.

Phase 2 — One chop, one base

~15–20 min
  1. Chop once for all three dishes: 3 onions, 4 tomatoes, ginger-garlic, chilies, cilantro.
  2. Cube 160 g paneer for the veg plates and keep it chilled.
  3. With only three dishes you can build one onion-tomato base and split it — it saves a pan and 15 minutes.

Phase 3 — Finish the three dishes

~25–35 min
  1. Kala chana: masala base + beans + amchur, simmer 15 min. ~900 g batch · 170 g portions.
  2. Arhar dal: ghee-cumin-garlic-onion tadka poured over the whisked dal. ~1.05 kg batch · 200 g portions.
  3. Aloo matar: masala → potatoes → covered 10 min → peas 5 min. ~650 g batch · 150 g portions.

Phase 4 — Portion & store

~15 min
  1. Cool everything uncovered 10–15 min — trapped steam makes watery fridge food.
  2. Box by meal at the halved plate size: 150–200 g curry + 110–120 g grain + 100–150 g yogurt. Label the boxes.
  3. Fourteen plates come off three batches (900 g + 1.05 kg + 650 g), so nothing sits in the fridge past Sunday.
  4. Chop salad veg for 2–3 days only; do the rest mid-week. Portion fruit for breakfasts.

Efficiency rule: never cook one pot at a time. The long cooks and the grain should all be running before you start the masala. Three dishes means one chopping board, one base, and one wash-up.

Daily tips

Reheat Splash of water on the curries. Weigh after reheating if you are tracking tightly.
Protein rule Legume or paneer dish + yogurt on every plate. 100 g Greek yogurt ≈ 10 g protein.
Half-plate template 150–200 g curry + 110–120 g grain + 100–150 g yogurt. That is the whole plan.
Still hungry? Add salad and yogurt volume before you add grain — it costs the fewest calories.
