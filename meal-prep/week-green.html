Core recipes

Batch on Sunday. Exact mix amounts below — scale with a kitchen scale when you can.

Palak Paneer

6 servings · ~1.8 kg batch
~380 kcal / serving ~300 g cooked

Spinach purée

  • 500 g spinach leaves (about 3 bunches, stems trimmed)
  • — boiling water to blanch + ice water to shock
  • 1 green chili (optional, blanched with spinach)

Gravy base

  • 2 tbsp oil + 1 tbsp butter/ghee optional
  • 1 large onion, chopped (~200 g)
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped (~250 g)
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 1–1¼ tsp salt
  • 120 ml water (½ cup), as needed
  • 2 tbsp cream or 3 tbsp milk (optional, for color)

Paneer

  • 350 g paneer, cut into 2 cm cubes
  • — optional: soak cubes in warm salted water 10 min so they stay soft

Method

  1. Blanch spinach 60–90 sec in boiling water; ice bath; squeeze lightly; blend with chili to a smooth purée (add 2–3 tbsp water if needed).
  2. Heat oil. Cumin seeds 20 sec → onion 6 min golden → ginger-garlic 1 min → tomato + dry spices 5–6 min until soft.
  3. Stir in spinach purée + ½ cup water. Simmer 4–5 min (don’t boil hard or color dulls).
  4. Add paneer cubes; simmer gently 4–5 min. Finish garam masala + cream/milk if using. Portion ~300 g (gravy + ~55–60 g paneer).

Includes ~55–60 g paneer per serving in gravy. Freeze purée separately if needed.

Kala Chana Masala

6 servings · ~1.5 kg batch
~310 kcal / serving ~250 g cooked

Beans

  • 450 g dry kala chana, soaked 8–12 hrs
  • 1 tsp salt
  • — water to cover for pressure-cooking

Masala

  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 2 large onions, finely chopped (~400 g)
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 3 medium tomatoes (~400 g), chopped
  • 2 tsp cumin powder
  • 2 tsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1–1½ tsp chili powder
  • 1½ tsp garam masala
  • 1 tsp amchur
  • 1–1½ tsp salt
  • 240 ml water (1 cup)

Method

  1. Pressure-cook soaked kala chana until soft (often longer than kabuli — 35–45 min). Keep 1 cup liquid.
  2. Golden onions in oil → ginger-garlic → tomato → all powders 30 sec.
  3. Add chana + water/liquid. Simmer 15–20 min; mash a few for body. Finish amchur + garam masala. Portion ~250 g.

From ~450 g dry kala chana. Very filling — 250 g curry is a solid protein base.

Moong Dal Tadka

7 servings · ~1.75 kg batch
~210 kcal / serving ~250 g cooked

Dal

  • 400 g yellow moong dal (about 2 cups)
  • 1.2 L water (5 cups)
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1½ tsp salt

Tadka (pour over at the end)

  • 2 tbsp ghee or oil
  • 1½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 dried red chilies
  • 1 pinch hing / asafoetida (optional)
  • ½ tsp red chili powder (off heat, optional)

Method

  1. Rinse moong well. Pressure-cook with water, turmeric, salt until soft (2–3 whistles / ~8–10 min). Whisk to a creamy but thick texture.
  2. In a small pan: heat ghee, cumin, dried chilies, garlic until garlic is golden (not burnt).
  3. Optional: kill heat, stir chili powder into the hot fat 5 sec.
  4. Pour tadka over dal; mix. Simmer 2 min. Portion ~250 g.

Lightest curry on this menu. Keep thick for meal prep — thin only when reheating.

Matar Paneer

6 servings · ~1.8 kg batch
~360 kcal / serving ~300 g cooked

Gravy

  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 2 large onions, chopped (~400 g)
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 3 medium tomatoes (~400 g) or 400 g purée
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1½ tsp cumin powder
  • 2 tsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 1–1½ tsp salt
  • 360 ml water (1½ cups)

Add-ins

  • 300 g paneer, 2 cm cubes
  • 300 g green peas (fresh or frozen — no need to thaw fully)
  • 1 tsp kasuri methi, crushed (optional)

Method

  1. Heat oil. Onions 8 min golden → ginger-garlic 1 min → tomato + turmeric/cumin/coriander 6–8 min until oil shows.
  2. Add 360 ml water; bring to a simmer. Add peas; cook 5–6 min until tender.
  3. Add paneer + salt + garam masala (+ kasuri methi). Simmer gently 5–7 min — don’t vigorously boil paneer.
  4. Portion ~300 g (gravy + peas + ~50 g paneer).

~50 g paneer + peas per serving. Frozen peas are fine and faster.

Lobia Masala

6 servings · ~1.5 kg batch
~280 kcal / serving ~250 g cooked

Beans

  • 400 g dry lobia / black-eyed peas, soaked 4–6 hrs (or 3 cans drained)
  • 1 tsp salt

Masala (same pattern as chana)

  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 2 large onions (~400 g), chopped
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 3 medium tomatoes (~400 g)
  • 2 tsp cumin powder
  • 2 tsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp chili powder
  • 1½ tsp garam masala
  • 1–1½ tsp salt
  • 240 ml water (1 cup)

Method

  1. Pressure-cook soaked lobia until soft (they cook faster than rajma — often 15–20 min). Reserve liquid.
  2. Build onion-tomato masala; add spices; add lobia + water. Simmer 12–15 min until saucy. Portion ~250 g.

From ~400 g dry black-eyed peas. Same base ratios as the chana/rajma batches.

Jeera Aloo

5 sides · ~750 g batch
~140 kcal / serving ~150 g cooked

In the pan

  • 700 g potatoes, boiled, peeled, 2 cm cubes
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 green chilies, slit
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ¾ tsp amchur
  • ¾–1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp coriander powder
  • 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped

Method

  1. Boil potatoes until just done; cube when cool enough.
  2. Heat oil; cumin until it crackles. Chili 10 sec. Add potatoes + turmeric + salt + coriander.
  3. Toss on medium-high 5–7 min until edges crisp slightly. Amchur + cilantro. Portion ~150 g.

North Indian cumin potatoes — replaces any South-style sides. No coconut, no mushrooms.

Sunday prep

Aim for ~2–2.5 hours. Soak legumes Saturday night; blanch spinach early so palak stays bright.

Saturday night

~5 min
  1. Soak 450 g kala chana and 400 g lobia (8–12 hrs). Skip if canned.
  2. Optional: wash 500 g spinach; spin dry and fridge.

Phase 1 — Long cooks in parallel

~10 min start · 30–45 min unattended
  1. Pressure-cook kala chana and lobia (separate pots or sequential if one cooker — chana first, it takes longer).
  2. Start moong dal: 400 g + 1.2 L water + turmeric + salt.
  3. Start quinoa/rice (4–5 cups dry → 150 g cooked portions).
  4. Boil 700 g potatoes for jeera aloo in a side pot.
  5. While those cook → Phase 2.

Phase 2 — Shared masala + spinach purée

~30 min
  1. Chop: 5–6 onions, 6–7 tomatoes, ginger-garlic, chilies. Cube all paneer (~700–800 g).
  2. Build a large onion-tomato masala; split into three bowls: kala chana / lobia / matar paneer.
  3. Blanch spinach 60–90 sec → ice bath → blend smooth for palak (bright green).

Phase 3 — Finish each dish (assembly line)

~40–50 min
  1. Finish kala chana and lobia in their masala bowls (15–20 min simmer each — stagger on two burners if you can).
  2. Whisk moong; pour tadka (ghee, cumin, garlic, dry chili). ~1.75 kg · 250 g portions.
  3. Palak paneer: cook purée with a small masala scrap → fold ~350 g paneer cubes 5 min. ~300 g portions.
  4. Matar paneer: peas + remaining masala + ~300–350 g paneer. ~300 g portions.
  5. Drain potatoes → jeera aloo 5–7 min (or fridge boiled potatoes and finish Mon in 10 min).

Phase 4 — Portion

~10–15 min
  1. Cool, then box for ~1850 kcal L+D days: curries 320–380 g, grain 230–250 g, yogurt 220–280 g.
  2. Keep one soft paneer pack aside if you want mid-week cubes without reheating gravy.

Efficiency rule: One masala base, three finishes — don’t make onion-tomato three separate times. Blanch spinach before you start heavy browning so it never waits in a hot pan.

Daily tips

Reheat Splash of water on curries — keeps gravy loose.
Protein rule Legume dish + yogurt or paneer. Weigh paneer cubes if tracking.
Palak color Don't over-boil spinach after blending; heat just enough to warm paneer.
Template Default plate: 250–300 g curry + 150 g grain + 150 g yogurt.
