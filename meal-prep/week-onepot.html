This week's three dishes

Every batch below is sized to exactly what the menu eats, plus one spare portion. Scale with a kitchen scale when you can.

Chana Masala

5 plates this week · ~900 g batch
~204 kcal / serving ~170 g on the plate

Chickpeas

  • 275 g dry kabuli chana, soaked 8 hrs (or 2 × 400 g cans, drained)
  • ½ tsp salt (for cooking)
  • 1 tea bag or ¼ tsp baking soda — optional, for softer chana

Masala base

  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped (~200 g)
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped or puréed (~250 g)
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp chana masala powder
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • ½ tsp amchur or chaat masala (optional)
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 150 ml water

Method

  1. Pressure-cook soaked chana with salt until soft but not mushy. Drain; keep ½ cup liquid.
  2. Golden onion in oil → ginger-garlic → tomato → spice powders 30 sec.
  3. Add chana + a splash of liquid. Simmer 15 min. Lightly crush a few chana for thickness.
  4. Finish with garam masala + amchur.

275 g dry chana makes the whole week's chana plates with one spare portion. Batch ~900 g — that is 5 plates of 170 g plus about 50 g spare.

High-Protein Khichdi

5 plates this week · ~1.32 kg batch
~300 kcal / serving ~250 g on the plate

Cook together (pot / Instant Pot)

  • 180 g mixed dal — moong + masoor
  • 105 g quinoa or rice
  • 900 ml water — add 100 ml more if you like it looser
  • ¾ tsp turmeric
  • 1¼ tsp salt

Temper

  • 1 tbsp ghee or oil
  • ¾ tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 small onion, chopped (~100 g)
  • 1 small tomato, chopped (~100 g)
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 green chili, slit

Finish

  • 75 g spinach, chopped
  • — serve each bowl with 150 g Greek yogurt

Method

  1. Rinse the dal + grain. Pressure-cook with water, turmeric and salt until soft and porridge-like (3–4 whistles / 10–12 min). Rest 5 min, then stir.
  2. In a small pan: ghee, cumin, then onion 4 min → ginger-garlic + chili 1 min → tomato 3 min.
  3. Fold the temper into the khichdi. Stir in the spinach until wilted. Adjust salt and water.
  4. Top with yogurt at serving, not in the batch.

This one carries its own grain — khichdi plates need no separate quinoa. Batch ~1.32 kg — that is 5 plates of 250 g plus about 75 g spare.

Matar Paneer

4 plates this week · ~775 g batch
~216 kcal / serving ~180 g on the plate

Gravy

  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 large onion, chopped (~175 g)
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes (~175 g) or 175 g purée
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ¾ tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 175 ml water

Add-ins

  • 125 g paneer, 2 cm cubes
  • 150 g green peas (fresh or frozen — no need to thaw fully)
  • ½ tsp kasuri methi, crushed (optional)

Method

  1. Heat oil. Onion 8 min golden → ginger-garlic 1 min → tomato + turmeric/cumin/coriander 6 min until the oil shows.
  2. Add 175 ml water; bring to a simmer. Add the peas; cook 5 min until tender.
  3. Add paneer + salt + garam masala (+ kasuri methi). Simmer gently 5 min — never boil paneer hard.

Peas and paneer together carry the protein; the gravy stays light. Batch ~775 g — that is 4 plates of 180 g plus about 55 g spare.

Simple Sautéed Spinach

Side · 4 sides · ~300 g leaves
~40 kcal / serving ~75 g cooked

In the pan

  • 300 g spinach leaves (about 1½ bunches, tough stems removed)
  • 4 tsp oil (1 tsp per serving)
  • ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 3 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 green chili, slit (optional)
  • ¼ tsp turmeric
  • ¼ tsp salt

Method

  1. Heat oil. Cumin 15 sec, then garlic (+ chili) until just golden — don't burn.
  2. Add the spinach in handfuls with turmeric and salt. Toss 2–3 min until wilted and most of the water evaporates.
  3. Make it fresh on the night — it takes three minutes and doesn't batch well.

Made to order, not batched on Sunday.

Sunday prep

About 60–75 minutes active for three dishes. Do the Saturday-night soaks so Sunday is mostly cook and portion.

Saturday night

~5 min
  1. Soak 275 g kabuli chana, 8 hrs.
  2. Optional: wash the salad veg and store it dry.

Phase 1 — Start the long cooks (in parallel)

~10 min to start · 30–45 min unattended
  1. Kabuli chana — pressure-cook until soft but not mushy.
  2. Khichdi — pressure-cook dal + grain together, 10–12 min.
  3. Put 350 g dry quinoa or rice on (≈ 1.1 kg cooked). The week needs 120 g cooked grain per plate.
  4. While the pots run → Phase 2. Don't stand around.

Phase 2 — One chop, one base

~15–20 min
  1. Chop once for all three dishes: 3 onions, 5 tomatoes, ginger-garlic, chilies, cilantro.
  2. Prep the paneer: 125 g for the matar paneer. Keep it chilled.
  3. With only three dishes you can build one onion-tomato base and split it — it saves a pan and 15 minutes.

Phase 3 — Finish the three dishes

~25–35 min
  1. Chana: masala base + chana masala powder + beans, simmer 15 min. ~900 g batch · 170 g portions.
  2. Khichdi: fold the temper in, wilt the spinach, loosen with water. ~1.32 kg batch · 250 g portions.
  3. Matar paneer: onion-tomato gravy → peas → paneer, gentle 5 min simmer. ~775 g batch · 180 g portions.

Phase 4 — Portion & store

~15 min
  1. Cool everything uncovered 10–15 min — trapped steam makes watery fridge food.
  2. Box by meal at the halved plate size: 170–250 g curry + 110–120 g grain + 100–150 g yogurt. Label the boxes.
  3. Fourteen plates come off three batches (900 g + 1.32 kg + 775 g), so nothing sits in the fridge past Sunday.
  4. Chop salad veg for 2–3 days only; do the rest mid-week. Portion fruit for breakfasts.

Efficiency rule: never cook one pot at a time. The long cooks and the grain should all be running before you start the masala. Three dishes means one chopping board, one base, and one wash-up.

Daily tips

Reheat Splash of water on the curries. Weigh after reheating if you are tracking tightly.
Protein rule Legume or paneer dish + yogurt on every plate. 100 g Greek yogurt ≈ 10 g protein.
Half-plate template 170–250 g curry + 110–120 g grain + 100–150 g yogurt. That is the whole plan.
Still hungry? Add salad and yogurt volume before you add grain — it costs the fewest calories.
