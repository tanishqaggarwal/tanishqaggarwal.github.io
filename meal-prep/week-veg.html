Core recipes

Batch on Sunday. Exact mix amounts below — scale with a kitchen scale when you can.

Bhindi Masala

6 sides · ~900 g batch
~120 kcal / serving ~150 g cooked

In the pan

  • 500 g bhindi (okra), washed, fully dried, 1.5 cm slices
  • 3 tbsp oil (bhindi needs a little more so it doesn’t slime)
  • 1 large onion, sliced (~200 g)
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped (~250 g)
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1–2 green chilies, slit
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ¾ tsp amchur or chaat masala
  • ¾–1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp garam masala

Method

  1. Pat bhindi bone-dry. Heat 2 tbsp oil in a wide pan; sauté bhindi on medium-high 8–10 min until edges brown and stickiness goes. Remove.
  2. Add 1 tbsp oil. Onion 5 min → ginger-garlic + chili 1 min → tomato + all powders except garam masala. Cook 4–5 min until soft.
  3. Return bhindi; toss 3–4 min. Finish garam masala. Portion ~150 g. Best slightly re-crisped when reheating.

Dry-ish North Indian bhindi — no coconut. Dry the okra well or it turns slimy.

Baingan Bharta

6 servings · ~1.2 kg batch
~140 kcal / serving ~200 g cooked

Eggplant

  • 1 kg large baingan (2–3 big), for roasting
  • 1 tsp oil to rub skins

Masala

  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped (~200 g)
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped (~250 g)
  • 1–2 green chilies, chopped
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ¾–1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • 2 tbsp chopped cilantro

Method

  1. Rub baingan with oil; roast over open flame or 220°C oven 35–45 min until collapsed and smoky. Cool, peel, mash flesh (~600–700 g mash).
  2. Heat oil. Onion 6 min golden → ginger-garlic + chili 1 min → tomato + powders 5 min until jammy.
  3. Add mashed baingan + salt. Cook 8–10 min on medium, stirring so it dries slightly and picks up smoky flavor.
  4. Garam masala + cilantro. Portion ~200 g. Pair with dal or paneer for protein.

Low calorie volume veg. Protein comes from the dal/paneer/yogurt you serve with it.

Kadai Paneer

6 servings · ~1.7 kg batch
~400 kcal / serving ~280 g cooked

Kadai masala (coarsely crush)

  • 1 tbsp coriander seeds
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 dry red chilies

Gravy + veg

  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 2 large onions — 1 chopped for gravy (~200 g), 1 in petals (~200 g)
  • 2 capsicums / bell peppers, 2 cm squares (~300 g) — no mushrooms
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 3 medium tomatoes, puréed (~400 g)
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp Kashmiri chili powder
  • 1–1½ tsp salt
  • 120 ml water (½ cup)
  • 1 tsp kasuri methi, crushed
  • ½ tsp garam masala

Paneer

  • 400 g paneer, 2 cm cubes

Method

  1. Dry-roast coriander, cumin, red chili 1–2 min; crush coarse (mortar or pulse).
  2. Heat oil. Cook chopped onion 7 min golden → ginger-garlic 1 min → tomato purée + turmeric + chili powder 8 min until thick.
  3. Add crushed kadai masala + ½ cup water; simmer 2 min.
  4. In another pan or same wide kadai: flash onion petals + capsicum 3–4 min on high (still crisp). Add to gravy.
  5. Add paneer, salt, kasuri methi, garam masala. Simmer gently 4–5 min. Portion ~280 g (~65 g paneer).

Capsicum only — no mushrooms. ~400 kcal per 280 g serving.

Punjabi Chole

6 servings · ~1.5 kg batch
~300 kcal / serving ~250 g cooked

Chickpeas

  • 450 g dry kabuli chana, soaked 8 hrs (or 3 cans drained)
  • 1 tea bag or 1 tsp chole masala in cook water for color (optional)
  • 1 tsp salt

Masala

  • 3 tbsp oil
  • 2 large onions, finely chopped (~400 g)
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 3 medium tomatoes (~400 g)
  • 2 tsp cumin powder
  • 2 tsp coriander powder
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1–2 tsp chole masala
  • 1 tsp amchur or anardana powder
  • 1–1½ tsp salt
  • 240 ml water (1 cup)
  • 1-inch ginger julienne to finish (optional)

Method

  1. Pressure-cook soaked chana until soft; keep 1 cup liquid.
  2. Golden onions → ginger-garlic → tomato → all powders 30 sec.
  3. Add chana + water/liquid. Simmer 15–20 min; mash a few. Finish amchur + ginger. Portion ~250 g.

Same protein role as Week 1 chana — darker Punjabi spice profile.

Arhar Dal Tadka

7 servings · ~1.75 kg batch
~220 kcal / serving ~250 g cooked

Dal

  • 400 g arhar / toor dal (about 2 cups)
  • 1.2 L water (5 cups)
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1½ tsp salt
  • 1 small tomato, chopped (optional in cooker)

Tadka

  • 2 tbsp ghee or oil
  • 1½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 pinch hing (optional)
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped (~150 g)
  • 1–2 dry red chilies or green chili
  • ½ tsp red chili powder (off heat, optional)

Method

  1. Rinse dal. Pressure-cook with water, turmeric, salt (+ tomato) until soft; whisk thick-creamy.
  2. Tadka: ghee → cumin → chili → garlic golden → onion 4 min. Optional chili powder off heat.
  3. Pour over dal; simmer 3 min. Portion ~250 g.

Classic North Indian arhar — not sambar. Keep thick for meal prep.

Jeera Aloo

5 sides · ~750 g batch
~140 kcal / serving ~150 g cooked

In the pan

  • 700 g potatoes, boiled, peeled, 2 cm cubes
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 green chilies, slit
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ¾ tsp amchur
  • ¾–1 tsp salt
  • ½ tsp coriander powder
  • 2 tbsp cilantro, chopped

Method

  1. Boil potatoes until just done; cube when cool enough to handle.
  2. Heat oil; cumin until it crackles. Chili 10 sec. Add potatoes + turmeric + salt + coriander.
  3. Toss on medium-high 5–7 min until edges crisp slightly. Amchur + cilantro. Portion ~150 g.

Simple North Indian side. Great with dal + yogurt when you want a break from bhindi.

Grab-and-go breakfasts

Pick one every morning. Weights are as prepared in the jar/bowl.

Sunday prep

Aim for ~2–2.5 hours. Roast baingan first (hands-off); cook bhindi last or Mon night so it stays crisp.

Saturday night

~5 min
  1. Soak 450 g kabuli chana for chole (8 hrs), or plan canned.
  2. Wash bhindi and air-dry completely overnight on a towel (critical — wet bhindi = slime).

Phase 1 — Oven + cooker at once

~15 min start · 35–45 min unattended
  1. Rub 1 kg baingan with oil; roast at 220°C (or open flame later). Set a timer.
  2. Pressure-cook chana for chole and start arhar dal (400 g + water + turmeric + salt).
  3. Start quinoa/rice and boil potatoes for jeera aloo.
  4. While those go → chop everything for Phase 2.

Phase 2 — Chop + two bases

~25 min
  1. Chop: onions, tomatoes, capsicum (no mushrooms), ginger-garlic, chilies. Cube 400–500 g paneer.
  2. Make chole masala base (onions deep golden — worth the time).
  3. Dry-roast and crush kadai spices (coriander + cumin + dry chili).
  4. When baingan is collapsed: peel, mash, fridge until gravy is ready.

Phase 3 — Finish proteins & dals

~40 min
  1. Simmer chole 15–20 min. ~250 g portions.
  2. Tadka on arhar dal; keep thick. ~250 g portions.
  3. Kadai paneer: gravy → flash capsicum + onion petals → paneer 4–5 min. ~280 g portions.
  4. Baingan bharta: onion-tomato → mash → cook dry 8–10 min. ~200 g portions.
  5. Bhindi: only if fully dry — sauté high heat then masala. Or slice Sunday and cook Mon dinner in 15 min for better texture.
  6. Optional now: jeera aloo from boiled potatoes (5–7 min).

Phase 4 — Portion

~10 min
  1. Cool → box for ~1850 kcal days: chole/dal 320–340 g, kadai paneer 320–350 g, grain 230–250 g, yogurt 220–250 g. Sabzi is extra volume.
  2. Yogurt cups 150–200 g. Salad veg for 2–3 days only.

Efficiency rule: Baingan in the oven + beans/dal on the stove is free parallel time. Bhindi is the one dish that can wait until Monday — everything else freezes/fridges well.

Daily tips

Protein rule Sabzi alone is light — always add dal, chole, paneer, or 150 g+ yogurt.
Bhindi Must be dry before cutting. Reheat in a pan, not soupy microwave.
Bharta Open-flame roast = best smoke. Oven works if you’re batching.
No mushrooms Kadai uses only capsicum + onion petals.
