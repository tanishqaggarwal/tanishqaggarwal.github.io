This week's three dishes

Every batch below is sized to exactly what the menu eats, plus one spare portion. Scale with a kitchen scale when you can.

Punjabi Chole

5 plates this week · ~875 g batch
~198 kcal / serving ~165 g on the plate

Chickpeas

  • 275 g dry kabuli chana, soaked 8 hrs (or 2 cans drained)
  • 1 tea bag or ½ tsp chole masala in the cooking water for colour (optional)
  • ½ tsp salt

Masala

  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped (~200 g)
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes (~250 g)
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp chole masala
  • ½ tsp amchur or anardana powder
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 150 ml water
  • 1-inch ginger, julienned, to finish (optional)

Method

  1. Pressure-cook soaked chana until soft; keep ½ cup liquid.
  2. Golden onion → ginger-garlic → tomato → all powders 30 sec.
  3. Add chana + liquid. Simmer 15 min; mash a few.
  4. Finish amchur + ginger julienne.

Darker Punjabi spice profile than plain chana masala — the two never land in the same week. Batch ~875 g — that is 5 plates of 165 g plus about 50 g spare.

Kadai Paneer

5 plates this week · ~850 g batch
~229 kcal / serving ~160 g on the plate

Kadai masala (coarsely crush)

  • 2 tsp coriander seeds
  • ½ tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 dry red chili

Gravy + veg

  • 1½ tbsp oil
  • 2 small onions — 1 chopped for the gravy (~100 g), 1 in petals (~100 g)
  • 1 large capsicum / bell pepper, 2 cm squares (~150 g) — no mushrooms
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes, puréed (~200 g)
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp Kashmiri chili powder
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 75 ml water
  • ½ tsp kasuri methi, crushed
  • ¼ tsp garam masala

Paneer

  • 200 g paneer, 2 cm cubes

Method

  1. Dry-roast the coriander, cumin and red chili 1–2 min; crush coarse.
  2. Heat oil. Chopped onion 7 min golden → ginger-garlic 1 min → tomato purée + turmeric + chili powder 6–8 min until thick.
  3. Add the crushed kadai masala + 75 ml water; simmer 2 min.
  4. Flash the onion petals + capsicum 3 min on high (keep them crisp). Add to the gravy.
  5. Add paneer, salt, kasuri methi, garam masala. Simmer gently 4 min.

Capsicum only — no mushrooms. About 40 g paneer per plated serving. Batch ~850 g — that is 5 plates of 160 g plus about 50 g spare.

Baingan Bharta

4 plates this week · ~650 g batch
~105 kcal / serving ~150 g on the plate

Eggplant

  • 550 g large baingan (1–2 big), for roasting
  • 1 tsp oil to rub the skins

Masala

  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped (~125 g)
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 medium tomato, chopped (~125 g)
  • 1 green chili, chopped
  • ¼ tsp turmeric
  • ¾ tsp cumin powder
  • ¾ tsp coriander powder
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp garam masala
  • 1 tbsp chopped cilantro

Method

  1. Rub the baingan with oil; roast over an open flame or at 220 °C for 35–45 min until collapsed and smoky. Cool, peel, mash the flesh (~350 g mash).
  2. Heat oil. Onion 6 min golden → ginger-garlic + chili 1 min → tomato + powders 5 min until jammy.
  3. Add the mashed baingan + salt. Cook 8 min on medium, stirring so it dries slightly.
  4. Garam masala + cilantro.

Low-calorie volume. The protein on this plate comes from the paneer cubes and yogurt beside it. Batch ~650 g — that is 4 plates of 150 g plus about 50 g spare.

Sunday prep

About 60–75 minutes active for three dishes. Do the Saturday-night soaks so Sunday is mostly cook and portion.

Saturday night

~5 min
  1. Soak 275 g kabuli chana, 8 hrs.
  2. Optional: wash the salad veg and store it dry.

Phase 1 — Start the long cooks (in parallel)

~10 min to start · 30–45 min unattended
  1. Chana for chole — pressure-cook with a tea bag for colour.
  2. Baingan — roast 35–45 min at 220 °C or over the flame.
  3. Put 550 g dry quinoa or rice on (≈ 1.6 kg cooked). The week needs 120 g cooked grain per plate.
  4. While the pots run → Phase 2. Don't stand around.

Phase 2 — One chop, one base

~15–20 min
  1. Chop once for all three dishes: 4 onions, 5 tomatoes, ginger-garlic, chilies, cilantro.
  2. Prep the paneer: 200 g for the kadai paneer, 160 g cubed for the veg plates. Keep it chilled.
  3. With only three dishes you can build one onion-tomato base and split it — it saves a pan and 15 minutes.

Phase 3 — Finish the three dishes

~25–35 min
  1. Chole: masala base + chole masala + chana, simmer 15 min, finish with ginger julienne. ~875 g batch · 165 g portions.
  2. Kadai paneer: crush the kadai masala, thicken the tomato base, flash the peppers, fold in paneer. ~850 g batch · 160 g portions.
  3. Bharta: mash the roasted flesh into the onion-tomato masala and dry it out 8 min. ~650 g batch · 150 g portions.

Phase 4 — Portion & store

~15 min
  1. Cool everything uncovered 10–15 min — trapped steam makes watery fridge food.
  2. Box by meal at the halved plate size: 150–165 g curry + 110–120 g grain + 100–150 g yogurt. Label the boxes.
  3. Fourteen plates come off three batches (875 g + 850 g + 650 g), so nothing sits in the fridge past Sunday.
  4. Chop salad veg for 2–3 days only; do the rest mid-week. Portion fruit for breakfasts.

Efficiency rule: never cook one pot at a time. The long cooks and the grain should all be running before you start the masala. Three dishes means one chopping board, one base, and one wash-up.

Daily tips

Reheat Splash of water on the curries. Weigh after reheating if you are tracking tightly.
Protein rule Legume or paneer dish + yogurt on every plate. 100 g Greek yogurt ≈ 10 g protein.
Half-plate template 150–165 g curry + 110–120 g grain + 100–150 g yogurt. That is the whole plan.
Still hungry? Add salad and yogurt volume before you add grain — it costs the fewest calories.
