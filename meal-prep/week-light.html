This week's three dishes

Every batch below is sized to exactly what the menu eats, plus one spare portion. Scale with a kitchen scale when you can.

Lobia Masala

5 plates this week · ~900 g batch
~190 kcal / serving ~170 g on the plate

Beans

  • 240 g dry lobia / black-eyed peas, soaked 4–6 hrs (or 2 cans drained)
  • ½ tsp salt

Masala

  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 1 large onion (~200 g), chopped
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes (~250 g)
  • 1 tsp cumin powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp chili powder
  • ¾ tsp garam masala
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • 150 ml water

Method

  1. Pressure-cook soaked lobia until soft — usually 15–20 min. Reserve the liquid.
  2. Build the onion-tomato masala; add the spice powders.
  3. Add lobia + water. Simmer 12–15 min until saucy.

Cooks in half the time of rajma — start it last on prep day. Batch ~900 g — that is 5 plates of 170 g plus about 50 g spare.

Moong Dal Tadka

5 plates this week · ~1.05 kg batch
~168 kcal / serving ~200 g on the plate

Dal

  • 240 g yellow moong dal
  • 720 ml water (about 3 cups)
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ¾ tsp salt

Tadka (pour over at the end)

  • 1 tbsp ghee or oil
  • ¾ tsp cumin seeds
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 dried red chili
  • 1 pinch hing / asafoetida (optional)
  • ¼ tsp red chili powder (off heat, optional)

Method

  1. Rinse the moong well. Pressure-cook with water, turmeric and salt until soft (2–3 whistles / ~8–10 min). Whisk creamy but thick.
  2. In a small pan: heat ghee, cumin, dried chili and garlic until the garlic is golden — not burnt.
  3. Optional: kill the heat and stir chili powder into the hot fat for 5 sec.
  4. Pour the tadka over the dal; mix and simmer 2 min.

Lightest curry in the rotation. Keep it thick for the fridge — thin only when reheating. Batch ~1.05 kg — that is 5 plates of 200 g plus about 50 g spare.

Bhindi Masala

4 plates this week · ~650 g batch
~120 kcal / serving ~150 g on the plate

In the pan

  • 350 g bhindi (okra), washed, fully dried, 1.5 cm slices
  • 2 tbsp oil (bhindi needs a little more so it doesn't slime)
  • 1 medium onion, sliced (~125 g)
  • 1 medium tomato, chopped (~125 g)
  • 2 tsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 green chili, slit
  • ¼ tsp turmeric
  • ¾ tsp cumin powder
  • ¾ tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp amchur or chaat masala
  • ¾ tsp salt
  • ¼ tsp garam masala

Method

  1. Pat the bhindi bone-dry. Heat 1½ tbsp oil in a wide pan; sauté the okra on medium-high 8–10 min until the edges brown and the stickiness goes. Remove.
  2. Add the rest of the oil. Onion 5 min → ginger-garlic + chili 1 min → tomato + all powders except garam masala. Cook 4 min until soft.
  3. Return the bhindi; toss 3 min. Finish with garam masala.

Dry the okra properly or it turns slimy. Re-crisp in a dry pan when reheating. Batch ~650 g — that is 4 plates of 150 g plus about 50 g spare.

Sunday prep

About 60–75 minutes active for three dishes. Do the Saturday-night soaks so Sunday is mostly cook and portion.

Saturday night

~5 min
  1. Soak 240 g lobia, 4–6 hrs.
  2. Optional: wash the salad veg and store it dry.

Phase 1 — Start the long cooks (in parallel)

~10 min to start · 30–45 min unattended
  1. Lobia — pressure-cook 15–20 min (fastest bean here).
  2. Moong dal — pressure-cook 8–10 min.
  3. Put 550 g dry quinoa or rice on (≈ 1.6 kg cooked). The week needs 120 g cooked grain per plate.
  4. While the pots run → Phase 2. Don't stand around.

Phase 2 — One chop, one base

~15–20 min
  1. Chop once for all three dishes: 2 onions, 3 tomatoes, ginger-garlic, chilies, cilantro.
  2. Cube 160 g paneer for the veg plates and keep it chilled.
  3. With only three dishes you can build one onion-tomato base and split it — it saves a pan and 15 minutes.

Phase 3 — Finish the three dishes

~25–35 min
  1. Lobia: masala base + beans + water, simmer 12 min. ~900 g batch · 170 g portions.
  2. Moong tadka: pour a fresh ghee-cumin-garlic tadka over the whisked dal. ~1.05 kg batch · 200 g portions.
  3. Bhindi masala: brown the okra dry first, then fold it back into the onion-tomato masala. ~650 g batch · 150 g portions.

Phase 4 — Portion & store

~15 min
  1. Cool everything uncovered 10–15 min — trapped steam makes watery fridge food.
  2. Box by meal at the halved plate size: 150–200 g curry + 110–120 g grain + 100–150 g yogurt. Label the boxes.
  3. Fourteen plates come off three batches (900 g + 1.05 kg + 650 g), so nothing sits in the fridge past Sunday.
  4. Chop salad veg for 2–3 days only; do the rest mid-week. Portion fruit for breakfasts.

Efficiency rule: never cook one pot at a time. The long cooks and the grain should all be running before you start the masala. Three dishes means one chopping board, one base, and one wash-up.

Daily tips

Reheat Splash of water on the curries. Weigh after reheating if you are tracking tightly.
Protein rule Legume or paneer dish + yogurt on every plate. 100 g Greek yogurt ≈ 10 g protein.
Half-plate template 150–200 g curry + 110–120 g grain + 100–150 g yogurt. That is the whole plan.
Still hungry? Add salad and yogurt volume before you add grain — it costs the fewest calories.
