Six things that decide your trip

Everything below this section is detail. These six are the trip.

1

Sleep warm

A 0 °F bag, a pad or air bed between you and the ground, and thermals to sleep in. Cold nights are the only reason people decide they hate camping.

2

Bring your own water

A gallon per person per day, plus extra for cooking and dishes. Assume the campsite has none — many don’t.

3

A headlamp each

There are no streetlights. One shared flashlight means one person can see and everyone else stands still.

4

Boil-water food only

Every meal here is boiled, wrapped in foil, or assembled cold. Nobody cooks well in the dark at 35 °F.

5

Layers, not one big coat

Thermals, fleece, jacket, hat, gloves. Desert and mountain nights drop 30 °F below the afternoon high.

6

Arrive before dark

Pitching a tent for the first time by headlamp is the classic first-trip mistake. Aim to be at camp two hours before sunset.

The meal plan

Six meals, two of them bought instead of cooked. The rule: if it needs more than a pot of boiling water or a sheet of foil, it doesn’t go on the trip.

Friday

Arrival day. Do not plan to cook.

🚗 Dinner

Grab it on the drive out

Eat on the road or pick something up at the last town before camp. Friday night is for putting up the tent, not for a first attempt at cooking outside.

Saturday

The full day — one boil in the morning, no cooking at lunch, fire at night.

🥣 Breakfast

Oatmeal + tea or instant coffee

Boil one pot of water. It makes the oatmeal and the drinks at the same time. Start it before anyone gets out of their sleeping bag.

Burritos

Tortillas, cheese, beans. Assembled cold in about four minutes, or warmed in foil on the stove. Easy to make in the morning and carry on a hike.

🔥 Dinner

Fire night

Potatoes wrapped in foil, buried in the coals for 45–60 minutes. Veggies alongside them. Popcorn in the covered pot at the end. Instant noodles are the backup if the fire won’t take.

Sunday

Pack-up day. One cooked meal, then let someone else feed you.

🍚 Breakfast

Poha + tea or instant coffee

Rinse the flattened rice, fry onion and potato, fold it through. One pan, ten minutes, and far better than a second morning of oatmeal.

🏪 Lunch

Camp store or lodge

Deliberately not cooked. You’ll be packing up a wet tent and reloading the car — buy lunch at the camp store or the nearest resort and eat it sitting down.

Bought
🌆 Dinner

Somewhere on the drive home

Stop wherever you get back to. Nobody wants to unload a car and cook on the same night.

No cooking

Two spare dinners: keep instant noodles and extra snacks in the car whatever the plan says. Fires get rained out, hikes run two hours long, and camp stores close earlier than you think.

Roughly how it goes

The shape of the weekend

Friday — drive and set up

Arrive before sunset
  1. Pick up the rental, load the car, buy ice last so it isn’t already melting.
  2. Drive. Eat on the way — nobody cooks the first night.
  3. Pitch the tent, inflate the bed, unroll the sleeping bags while it’s still light.
  4. Set out headlamps and lanterns before dark, then sit down and do nothing.

Saturday — the whole day out

Big day
  1. Boil water, oatmeal and coffee, out of camp early.
  2. Build burritos before you leave and carry them with you.
  3. Hike or drive to whatever you came to see. Take more water than feels necessary.
  4. Back at camp with daylight to spare — a fire takes 45 minutes to become coals.
  5. Potatoes in the coals, veggies, popcorn, then stars.

Sunday — one more thing, then home

Half day
  1. Poha and coffee, then break camp — it always takes longer than setting up.
  2. Dry the tent in the sun for ten minutes if you can; packing it wet ruins it.
  3. Do one short stop on the way out rather than a big morning plan.
  4. Lunch at the camp store, drive home, dinner somewhere on the road.

Rookie mistakes

The ones everyone makes exactly once.

Cold from below The ground steals more heat than the air. A pad or air bed under you matters more than a thicker blanket over you.
Dead phones Cold batteries drop to nothing fast. Sleep with your phone and your charger inside the sleeping bag.
No signal, no map Download maps, the playlist and your reservation before you leave the last town with bars.
Food left out Everything scented goes in the car or a locker overnight — food, trash, toothpaste, sunscreen.
Fire rules Check restrictions the week of. Burn only local wood, and drown the fire until you can touch the ashes.
Leave no trace Carry out every bit of trash, including the peels. Leave the site cleaner than the person before you did.
